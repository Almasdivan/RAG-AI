Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique used in artificial intelligence to improve the accuracy and reliability of responses generated by Large Language Models (LLMs). Instead of depending only on the information stored during the model’s training, RAG retrieves relevant information from external sources such as documents, databases, websites, or knowledge bases. This retrieved information is then provided to the AI model as context, allowing it to generate a more relevant and informative answer.

The RAG process generally involves three main steps: retrieval, augmentation, and generation. First, the system searches a knowledge source and retrieves information related to the user’s question. Next, the retrieved information is added to the user’s query as additional context. Finally, the language model uses this context to generate the final response. RAG is widely used in applications such as AI chatbots, customer support systems, document-based question answering, and enterprise knowledge assistants because it can help reduce hallucinations and provide answers based on up-to-date or domain-specific information.
